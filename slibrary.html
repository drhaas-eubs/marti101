Westerman, Bonnet & McAfee

Digital Maturity and the Discipline of Leading Digital

George Westerman · Didier Bonnet · Andrew McAfee · MIT & Capgemini

A maturity model mapping firms on two axes — digital intensity and transformation-management intensity — into Beginners, Conservatives, Fashionistas and Digirati.

1. The “Why” — Why Westerman, Why Now?

Before leading a transformation you need to know where your firm actually stands. This framework gives Unit 1 its diagnostic anchor: digital ambition is worthless without the leadership capability to execute it.

2. Intellectual Foundation

From a study of 400+ global firms by MIT's Center for Digital Business and Capgemini Consulting. Westerman is a research scientist at the MIT Initiative on the Digital Economy; Bonnet led practices at Capgemini; McAfee co-directs the MIT IDE.

3. The Frameworks · Tools · Models
Digital Maturity MatrixDigital intensity × transformation-management intensity → four maturity types
4. Operationalisation — How to Use It
  • Score your firm on digital intensity
  • Score on transformation-management intensity
  • Locate your quadrant (Beginner/Conservative/Fashionista/Digirati)
  • Close the weaker axis first
  • Re-baseline as capability grows
[ Insert portrait sketch ]

“Technology changes quickly, but organizations change much more slowly. ⚠”

— Westerman, Bonnet & McAfee

5. Limitations — The Critical View
  • Two-axis simplification of a complex reality
  • Snapshot, not a dynamic path
  • Sample skewed to large incumbents
  • Says less about *how* to build leadership capability
6. Enduring Impact
  • Coined 'Digirati' and the maturity vocabulary
  • Foundation text for corporate DT programmes
  • Widely used consulting diagnostic
  • Direct anchor for MARTI101 Unit 1

Hans Moravec

Moravec's Paradox — Hard Is Easy, Easy Is Hard

Carnegie Mellon Robotics Institute · b. 1948

The observation that high-level reasoning needs little computation, while low-level sensorimotor skills — trivial for humans — demand enormous resources.

1. The “Why” — Why Hans Moravec, Why Now?

Intuition misleads leaders about what AI finds hard. Moravec's Paradox recalibrates expectations — the reality check of Unit 1.

2. Intellectual Foundation

Hans Moravec is a robotics pioneer at Carnegie Mellon's Robotics Institute. The paradox was articulated in Mind Children (1988) alongside Minsky, Rodney Brooks and others.

3. The Frameworks · Tools · Models
Moravec's ParadoxHard for humans is easy for machines — and vice versa
4. Operationalisation — How to Use It
  • Question intuitions about 'easy' vs 'hard' AI tasks
  • Expect strength in reasoning, weakness in dexterity
  • Plan for the sensorimotor bottleneck
  • Set realistic automation expectations
[ Insert portrait sketch ]

“It is comparatively easy to make computers exhibit adult-level performance on intelligence tests, and difficult to give them the skills of a one-year-old. ⚠”

— Hans Moravec

5. Limitations — The Critical View

  • Boundaries shift as robotics advances
  • Often cited loosely
6. Enduring Impact

A critique arguing that large language models produce fluent text by statistical pattern-matching without understanding, carrying risks of bias, environmental cost and false coherence.

1. The “Why” — Why Bender, Why Now?

AI hype needs a counterweight. This paper gives Unit 1 the critical vocabulary for the limits, costs and risks of large models.

2. Intellectual Foundation

Emily M. Bender (linguist, University of Washington), Timnit Gebru (then Google, now DAIR), Angelina McMillan-Major and Margaret Mitchell authored the landmark FAccT 2021 paper — famous also for the dismissal that followed at Google.

3. The Frameworks · Tools · Models
Stochastic Parrots — Model LimitsFluency above the waterline, no understanding below
4. Operationalisation — How to Use It
  • Separate fluency from understanding in any AI output
  • Audit training data for bias and provenance
  • Account for environmental and financial cost
  • Keep humans accountable for model claims

“An LM is a system for haphazardly stitching together sequences of linguistic forms … without any reference to meaning: a stochastic parrot. ⚠”
